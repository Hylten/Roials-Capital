Too many equity-heavy structures.

PHASE 2: TECHNICAL MECHANICS

Balance sheet authority is a function of mechanics, not charisma. Private credit responds to two variables above all others: behavior of collateral and predictability of recovery. When Roials-Capital engineers a liquidity model for a GP or family office, the first analysis focuses on isolating the core asset class. Not the broad portfolio. The specific nucleus that can anchor a secured facility.

The operational logic rests on five mechanics.

1. The LTV curve. Lenders do not price at a single point. They view collateral on a spectrum from static to kinetic. Hard assets with stable valuation histories command a higher kinetic rating. That rating influences whether the LTV can sit at 40 percent, 55 percent, or 70 percent. The GP that understands this curve knows when to lean into leverage and when to avoid it. A GP who does not understand it goes into negotiation blind.

2. Cash flow waterfalls. The strongest operators define waterfalls before a lender ever requests them. Priority must be unambiguous. Senior secured sits at the top. Operating cash flow follows. Equity distributions come last. A lender reviewing a pre-architected waterfall increases credit appetite. The friction drops. The pricing improves.

3. Recovery factors. This is the silent metric. Lenders run internal stress tests to determine the percentage of value recoverable under a forced sale or workout scenario. If the GP cannot articulate the recovery logic, the lender prices for uncertainty. Roials-Capital builds recovery maps in advance. Every asset class has a liquidation velocity. Every jurisdiction has a disposal friction. The map must reflect this.

4. Cross collateral interaction. A modern balance sheet often contains assets that should not be bundled. Mixing operational assets with legacy real estate is a structural error. Segregation increases lending appetite because it prevents cross contamination of risk. Senior lenders reward clarity of isolation. Junior lenders reward clearly defined secondary coverage. The entire capital stack stabilizes.

5. Covenant design. Loose covenants communicate operational weakness. Excessively tight covenants freeze tactical movement. The correct design creates a controlled corridor for decision making. The lender sees discipline. The GP gains operational freedom without violating thresholds.

These mechanics are not theoretical. They determine how quickly a Fund-III raise closes. They determine whether the GP retains negotiation leverage. They determine whether the LP views the GP as an allocator or an operator. A GP with technical command earns silent authority. The balance sheet becomes a signaling device. Not a liability.
