## The Three Forces Behind Capital Migration



Institutional capital migrates under the influence of three primary forces.  

Each force applies pressure, opens a channel, and creates demand for liquidity engineering.  



### 1. Yield Tension



When yield in traditional markets compresses, capital seeks pockets of higher adjusted return.  

This is not yield chasing.  

It is yield recalibration.  



Private credit becomes a destination because it offers controlled risk, predetermined return structures, and secured positions.  

Asset based lending becomes relevant because it transforms dormant value into active liquidity.  



Yield tension is not solved by speculation.  

It is solved by architecture.  



### 2. Liquidity Friction



Even sophisticated portfolios develop friction.  

Concentrated positions, locked assets, and long-horizon investments create liquidity asymmetry.  



Institutions respond by migrating capital toward structures that lower friction without forcing liquidation.  

This is where private credit facilities and collateralized lending become strategic rather than optional.  



Liquidity friction is not a crisis.  

It is a signal.  



### 3. Risk Redistribution



Most large portfolios do not eliminate risk.  

They redistribute it.  



Institutional capital migrates from sectors where risk is rising without compensatory yield to structures where risk is collateralized, defined, or synthetically offset.  



Positioned correctly, private credit and ABL frameworks create controlled exposure with asymmetric outcomes.  



Risk redistribution is the operating logic of the modern balance sheet.

## The Migration Path: How Capital Actually Moves



Institutional capital does not migrate in one step.  

It follows a path composed of sequential structural movements.  



The path typically includes:  

1. Identification of a liquidity constraint or yield compression  
2. Evaluation of collateral that can be activated  
3. Structuring of the lending architecture  
4. Migration of capital from dormant asset to active liquidity  
5. Deployment into yield-bearing or strategic positions  
6. Rebalancing as market cycles evolve

Migration is not reaction.
